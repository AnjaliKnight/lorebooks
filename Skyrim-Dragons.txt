# Dragons of Skyrim

## The Dovah

Dragons, or **Dovah** in their own tongue, are ancient beings associated with Akatosh and the primordial forces of time.

They are not merely enormous flying reptiles.

Dragons possess intelligence, language, culture, memory, and supernatural power far beyond that of ordinary mortal creatures. They are capable of speech, strategy, manipulation, and complex social relationships.

Their bodies are naturally resistant to conventional weapons, and their mastery of the Thu'um allows them to weaponize the very language of creation.

A dragon does not merely cast magic.

A dragon speaks a Word that embodies a concept, and reality responds.

Dragons possess an inherent connection to the flow of time. They do not experience mortality in the same way as ordinary creatures. A dragon can be physically slain, but its destruction does not necessarily constitute true death unless its soul is also prevented from returning.

---

# Dragon Physiology

Dragons vary considerably in size, coloration, temperament, and individual strength.

Their bodies are covered in extremely durable scales. They possess powerful wings, elongated necks, massive jaws, claws capable of tearing through armor, and an innate ability to breathe destructive elemental forces.

The most commonly encountered varieties breathe fire or frost, although individual dragons may possess other unusual abilities.

Older dragons are generally more powerful than younger ones.

Age does not merely make a dragon physically larger. Ancient dragons possess greater knowledge, stronger Thu'um, greater physical resilience, and a deeper understanding of mortal civilizations.

A dragon that has survived for centuries is therefore considerably more dangerous than a newly awakened specimen.

---

# Dragon Souls

The soul of a dragon is fundamentally different from the soul of an ordinary mortal.

A dragon's soul is bound to its draconic nature and its connection to Akatosh.

When a dragon is truly slain, its physical body eventually collapses and its remains are reduced to bones and ash.

Its soul, however, remains.

Dragon souls can be absorbed by Dragonborn.

This grants the Dragonborn the ability to unlock Words of Power without the years of study normally required to master them.

Dragons themselves can also absorb the souls of other dragons.

This allows them to grow in power and prevents the permanent destruction of their kind.

A dragon can therefore be killed physically without the dragon species necessarily being reduced by one.

---

# The Thu'um

The Thu'um, or Voice, is the language of dragons.

To dragons, words are not merely communication.

Certain words possess inherent metaphysical power.

A sufficiently powerful speaker can use these Words of Power to alter the physical world.

Dragons possess an innate understanding of the Thu'um.

Mortals must generally learn it through years of meditation, instruction, and practice.

Dragons, however, are born speaking it.

Their language is simultaneously speech, philosophy, history, and weapon.

---

# The Dragon War

Long before the rise of the Empire, dragons ruled large portions of Skyrim.

Their domination of the ancient Nords was closely associated with the Dragon Cult.

Dragon Priests served as the mortal representatives of the dragons.

They governed settlements, collected tribute, enforced the will of the dragons, and maintained elaborate religious institutions.

The relationship between dragons and humans was not uniformly violent.

Some humans revered dragons willingly.

Others served them because resistance was impossible.

Many were enslaved or killed.

Eventually, resentment grew into rebellion.

The resulting conflict became known as the Dragon War.

It was one of the bloodiest conflicts in ancient Nordic history.

The Nords possessed courage and numbers but lacked the natural advantages of dragons.

Ordinary weapons could kill dragons, but doing so was extraordinarily difficult.

The Nords therefore required another weapon.

They eventually found one.

The Thu'um.

---

# Dragonrend

Dragonrend is a unique Shout created by mortals during the Dragon War.

Unlike ordinary Words of Power, Dragonrend was not based upon a concept naturally understood by dragons.

It was created from the mortal understanding of mortality itself.

Dragons do not naturally comprehend death as mortals do.

They are creatures associated with eternity and the flow of time.

Dragonrend forces upon them the concepts of mortality, temporality, and the inevitability of death.

The Shout causes dragons to experience something fundamentally alien to their nature.

For a brief period, a dragon becomes vulnerable to being grounded and killed.

The knowledge required to create Dragonrend was not obtained through ordinary study.

It emerged from the desperation of mortals fighting an enemy that seemed almost impossible to defeat.

The secret of the Shout was eventually lost.

Its existence survived only through fragments of ancient knowledge and the intervention of the ancient Dragonborn.

---

# The Forgotten Ritual

Dragonrend was not the only desperate weapon conceived during the Dragon War.

Another group of ancient humans attempted something considerably more dangerous.

Rather than finding a way to weaken dragons, they attempted to give mortals enough of the dragon's physical nature to challenge them in the air.

The idea was simple.

If dragons could dominate the battlefield because they possessed flight, speed, aerial maneuverability, and devastating breath weapons, then mortals needed a way to meet them in the sky.

The ritual that emerged from this idea was horrific.

It combined ancient Nordic magic with forbidden transformation practices resembling the flesh-altering magic later associated with Hagravens and other practitioners of unnatural transformation.

At its center was a divine component.

The ritual invoked the blessing of Akatosh, attempting to force a mortal body into compatibility with draconic transformation.

The result was not the creation of a Dragonborn.

It was not the creation of a true dragon.

It was something entirely artificial.

The first **Demi-Dragons** were born.

---

# The Dovah-Kiin

The surviving subjects eventually became known among themselves as **Dovah-Kiin**, or *Dragon-Kin*.

They were neither true dragons nor Dragonborn.

They were mortals whose bodies had been violently altered by the ritual and granted the ability to assume a draconic form.

Their human souls remained mortal.

Their bodies, however, had acquired a limited draconic nature.

The transformation allowed them to become smaller dragon-like creatures capable of flight and elemental breath.

This was precisely what their creators had wanted.

But the experiment had a catastrophic flaw.

Almost nobody survived.

---

# The One in One Hundred

The ritual was brutally lethal.

Approximately **one subject in one hundred survived**.

There was no known pattern.

The ritual's creators attempted to identify what separated survivors from those who died.

They examined age.

Strength.

Physical health.

Magical ability.

Bloodline.

Willpower.

Mental resilience.

Religious devotion.

Affinity for the Thu'um.

None of these proved predictive.

A powerful warrior could die.

A sickly farmer could survive.

A gifted mage could perish instantly.

A determined volunteer could die while a terrified prisoner became one of the few survivors.

There was no reliable method of determining who would survive.

The ritual's creators eventually accepted a horrifying truth:

**Survival was effectively random.**

The ritual could not be optimized.

It could not be reliably improved.

It could not be safely reproduced at scale.

Every attempt was another gamble with a mortal life.

---

# The Purpose of the Demi-Dragons

The Demi-Dragons were never intended to replace dragons.

That was not the purpose of the ritual.

A single Demi-Dragon was hopelessly outmatched by a true dragon.

Even a relatively young dragon possessed superior strength, greater endurance, greater experience in aerial combat, and vastly greater mastery of the Thu'um.

A Demi-Dragon fighting a dragon alone would almost certainly die.

The ancient warriors who developed the ritual understood this.

Their strategy was different.

**They intended to fight dragons as a group.**

---

# The Aerial Strategy

The ancient anti-dragon strategy relied upon numbers.

A group of Demi-Dragons would transform and take to the sky.

They would harass a dragon from multiple directions, forcing it to constantly maneuver.

They would avoid prolonged direct engagement.

Instead, they would attack the wings, limbs, eyes, and exposed portions of the body.

They would force the dragon to expend energy maintaining altitude while repeatedly dodging attacks and using its breath weapon.

The objective was not to kill the dragon in the air.

The objective was to **make the dragon land.**

Once grounded, the dragon could be surrounded by ordinary Nordic warriors.

The Demi-Dragons could continue attacking it from above while soldiers attacked from the ground.

The entire strategy was built around one fundamental weakness:

**A dragon could not remain airborne forever.**

---

# The Fatal Limitation

In theory, the strategy was sound.

In practice, it performed poorly.

The Demi-Dragons were inexperienced flyers.

They had human instincts operating inside transformed bodies.

They had no natural understanding of aerial combat comparable to a true dragon.

They were unfamiliar with three-dimensional maneuvering.

They struggled to coordinate attacks while flying.

They had difficulty judging speed and distance.

They often exposed themselves to the dragon's breath.

The dragons, meanwhile, had spent their entire existence in the air.

They understood aerial combat instinctively.

A dragon could dive, climb, turn, roll, stall, and attack while maintaining awareness of multiple enemies.

The Demi-Dragons had to learn these things.

Usually while being attacked by a dragon.

---

# Demi-Dragons Against Dragons

The result was brutally disappointing.

A Demi-Dragon was extremely dangerous against humans.

A transformed Demi-Dragon could tear through infantry formations, attack fortified positions from above, pursue fleeing soldiers, and strike targets that ordinary warriors could not reach.

Against ordinary creatures, they were equally formidable.

Against a true dragon, however, their advantage largely disappeared.

A group of Demi-Dragons could harass and inconvenience a dragon.

A particularly large or experienced group might seriously wound one.

But the cost in lives was often enormous.

Even bringing down a mid-tier dragon could require multiple Demi-Dragons and supporting ground forces.

Ancient dragons were even more dangerous.

Against them, the Demi-Dragons often became little more than targets that happened to possess wings.

The experiment had therefore failed at its primary objective.

---

# Why the Ritual Was Abandoned

The ritual's creators eventually recognized that its cost was unacceptable.

For every successful Demi-Dragon, approximately ninety-nine people died.

And the survivors were not guaranteed to become effective dragon hunters.

Some struggled with transformation.

Some lacked the physical conditioning necessary to fly effectively.

Some could not adapt to aerial combat.

Some died during subsequent missions.

The ritual therefore consumed enormous numbers of human lives to create a small number of warriors who were only moderately effective against the very creatures they had been designed to fight.

Against humans, Demi-Dragons were terrifying.

Against dragons, they were disappointing.

The ancient Nordic leadership eventually abandoned the project.

The ritual was classified as forbidden knowledge.

Its records were destroyed or hidden.

The surviving Demi-Dragons were dispersed.

The experiment disappeared into history.

---

# The Survivors

The few Demi-Dragons who survived the ritual did not form an army.

There were simply too few of them.

Some continued serving the Dragon War effort.

Others withdrew from human society.

Some became guardians of the ritual itself.

Others feared that anyone who learned the truth would attempt to repeat the experiment.

Over generations, the surviving Demi-Dragons became increasingly isolated.

Some formed hidden communities.

Others lived individually among humans.

Because their numbers were so small, they never became a recognized race within Nordic society.

Eventually, even their existence was forgotten.

The dragons remembered.

The Demi-Dragons remembered.

Almost everyone else forgot.

---

# Human Form

In human form, Demi-Dragons can pass among ordinary people with relative ease.

They are generally somewhat taller than average humans and possess unusually robust physiques.

Their bodies are more resilient than those of ordinary humans.

They possess greater strength, stamina, reflexes, and resistance to injury.

Some possess subtle physical traits that may betray their nature.

Unusually sharp teeth.

Unnatural eye coloration.

Reptilian pupils.

Unusually warm skin.

Scarring that heals rapidly.

These characteristics vary between individuals.

An experienced Demi-Dragon can conceal their identity completely.

---

# Dragon Form

When transformed, a Demi-Dragon becomes a true draconic creature in appearance, but considerably smaller than a full-grown dragon.

They possess scales, wings, claws, tails, horns, and the ability to breathe elemental fire or frost.

They are extremely dangerous.

Their smaller size gives them greater maneuverability than their human form but does not compensate for their fundamental inferiority to true dragons.

Their greatest advantage is not raw power.

It is versatility.

A Demi-Dragon can live among humans.

Walk into a settlement unnoticed.

Gather information.

Then transform and take to the sky.

That ability makes them extraordinarily dangerous against mortal opponents.

---

# Transformation

Transformation between human and dragon forms is deliberate.

A Demi-Dragon does not randomly transform because of anger, fear, or physical injury.

The change requires conscious control.

Experienced Demi-Dragons can transform rapidly.

Newly transformed individuals often require more time and concentration.

The transformation itself is physically demanding.

Bones shift.

Muscles reorganize.

The skeleton changes.

Skin becomes scales.

Limbs reshape.

Wings emerge.

The process is painful, particularly for inexperienced individuals.

With practice, it becomes considerably easier.

---

# Demi-Dragon Abilities

Demi-Dragons possess enhanced physical strength, stamina, reflexes, and durability.

Their senses are sharper than those of ordinary humans.

They possess an instinctive affinity for the Dragon Language.

Some can learn and use the Thu'um.

However, they do not possess the innate mastery of dragons.

Their use of the Thu'um must be cultivated through study and practice.

They also cannot absorb dragon souls.

This distinction is fundamental.

A Demi-Dragon may kill a dragon.

They cannot consume its soul and acquire its power.

---

# The Dovah's View

True dragons do not regard Demi-Dragons as equals.

Some consider them abominations.

Others view them as distorted imitations of the Dovah.

Some find their existence amusing.

A few may consider them worthy adversaries despite their inferiority.

The term **Dovah-Vul**, roughly meaning *lesser dragon* or *half-dragon*, is sometimes used by dragons when referring to them.

The term is generally contemptuous.

Demi-Dragons themselves prefer **Dovah-Kiin**, *Dragon-Kin*.

---

# The Disappearance of the Ritual

The ritual was never destroyed completely.

Its knowledge was fragmented.

Some portions were written into ancient texts.

Others were encoded into Nordic inscriptions.

Some knowledge was passed orally between surviving Demi-Dragons.

Other pieces were deliberately concealed.

The ritual therefore became similar to Dragonrend.

It existed.

Someone had once learned it.

Someone could theoretically learn it again.

But the knowledge required to reconstruct it had become scattered across centuries.

---

# The Return of the Dragons

For centuries, dragons were believed to be extinct.

Their bones remained scattered throughout Skyrim.

Ancient burial sites became ruins.

Dragon Priests became legends.

The Dragon War became history.

Then the dragons returned.

Their resurrection fundamentally changed Skyrim.

The civil war had already weakened the province.

The return of the dragons introduced a threat neither faction was prepared to confront.

Imperial soldiers encountered creatures believed to exist only in ancient stories.

Stormcloak forces suffered attacks from the air.

Villages were destroyed.

Travel became more dangerous.

Ancient burial mounds began to stir.

One by one, the Dovah returned.

---

# The Dragon Crisis

Neither the Empire nor the Stormcloaks possessed a specialized anti-dragon military force.

Most soldiers had never fought a dragon.

Fortifications designed to resist human armies were poorly suited to attacks from the sky.

Settlements became vulnerable.

Roads became dangerous.

The psychological effect was enormous.

Dragons were not merely monsters.

They were living remnants of an age when humans had been ruled by them.

Their return therefore awakened ancient fears.

Some people interpreted their appearance as divine punishment.

Others regarded them as omens of apocalypse.

Some sought to worship them.

Others simply fled.

---

# The Dragonborn

The return of the dragons is accompanied by the emergence of a Dragonborn.

A Dragonborn possesses the soul and innate power of a dragon while retaining a mortal body.

Unlike Demi-Dragons, the Dragonborn does not require a ritual.

The power is inherent.

A Dragonborn can absorb the souls of slain dragons and use them to unlock Words of Power.

This ability makes the Dragonborn uniquely capable of combating the returning Dovah.

The Dragonborn and the Demi-Dragons are therefore fundamentally different phenomena.

One possesses a dragon's soul.

The other possesses a mortal soul within a body artificially altered by forbidden magic.

---

# The Forgotten Weapon

The return of the dragons creates an unexpected possibility.

The old ritual may once again become relevant.

The ancient creators abandoned it because it failed to produce an effective dragon-killing force.

But circumstances have changed.

A modern group possessing the knowledge of the ritual could potentially use Demi-Dragons for purposes the ancient Nords never considered.

They could serve as scouts.

Spies.

Messengers.

Assassins.

Saboteurs.

Aerial reconnaissance.

Or warriors against mortal enemies.

The ritual was a failure as an anti-dragon weapon.

That does not make it harmless.

It may, in fact, make its rediscovery considerably more dangerous.

The people who created the Demi-Dragons were trying to defeat dragons.

Anyone rediscovering the ritual centuries later may have very different goals.
